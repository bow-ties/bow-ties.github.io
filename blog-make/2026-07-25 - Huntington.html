2026-07-05 - The Huntington Library, Art Museum and Botanical Gardens

During the 2026 AAPT Summer Conference, the Community on the History and Philosophy of Physics hosted a trip to the Huntington Library for a tour led by Joel A. Klein, Molina Curator for the History of Medicine and Allied Sciences. Their main building is under renovations so none of the big ticket items were on display. Notably, nothing from the Burndy Library (which spent 15 years at MIT until being demolished to expand Sloan) with writings from Newton, Einstein, Copernicus, Hooke, etc. and electrical apparatus. However, they will soon move into 4000 sqft of science exhibit space circa 2029, and the first exhibit has a book available, Worlds Unfolding: Science on the Page.
Instead, we saw two small exhibits on the moon and damaged goods. I then spent the following 6 hours wandering the museums and gardens.



Notably, I took a selfie with Blinking Sam:



I heard that one of their most famous paintings was "Blue Boy", but I ultimately walked out with the question of who wore it better?

  • François Hubert Drouais - Boy with Peaches, 1760
  • Thomas Gainsborough - The Blue Boy, 1770
  • Mark Gertler - Portrait of a Girl in a Blue Jersey (Carrington), 1912



Later, I wandered through the alien planet known as the desert garden in search of a euphrobia lactea cousin of Henry Kendall's cactus, which we maintain at MIT. Sadly, I could only find a vague set of unidentified candelabra-style tree-climbing plants.



I eventually settled in the lily pad garden with turtles, fish, and dragonflies.
